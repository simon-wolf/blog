Just under 20 years ago my dad set up a business which sold small GPS-enabled personal alarms to elderly people who still lived independently but wanted the peace of mind that they could press a button and talk to someone in an emergency response centre thanks to a multi-network SIM card in the device.

Five and a half years ago dad passed away after a short illness and my sister and I took over Mindme. Dad's death had a weird effect on me and I became overly obsessed about my life expectancy. It took almost four years to make changes but at the start of 2025 I started to eat much more healthily, lose weight and, for the first time in my life, I started to regularly exercise.

One of the things I started doing was running. I am fortunate in that I live in a small village in Somerset so I have quiet country lanes and plenty of off-road trails to explore. But that is a double-edged sword.

I have always had a fear in the back of my mind that if I had a bad fall or, worse, was hit by a car which didn't stop, I could be badly injured and lie undiscovered for hours or even days. I live alone so nobody at home would immediately notice me not getting back. I'd be relying on my daughter worrying when messages or calls were unanswered. But even once the alarm was raised, nobody would know where I am or even that I'd gone out for a run.

Whilst I take my mobile phone with me it doesn't always have signal and it could easily break if I had a bad fall. I don't routinely share my location, both because I value my privacy but also because I simply forget to turn in on or off. And if I do call someone for help, I have to hope that they answer. And none of this helps if I was unconscious.

So I started carrying one of our devices. The multi-network SIM increases the chances of getting a network connection as I move between cell masts. If I trigger the alarm I know that someone will answer because they are in a 24/7 response centre. And they can immediately see where I am because their call management system integrates with our platform.

But that still doesn't solve the risk of me lying somewhere unconscious or just groggy after a fall.

The solution is based on the fact that our platform can detect when a device leaves or returns home. When it leaves we start checking the device's data regularly. Each time it is checked we see if the device has moved more than a few metres in the previous few minutes. If it hasn't then we alert the response centre and they will make a phone call to my mobile phone or to the device itself to check if I am okay.

And then it occurred to me that I cannot be the only person in this situation. So we decided to launch this as a new service anyone can take advantage of.

But other people face other dangers. People who run in towns and cities may worry more about the people they encounter so we are including a silent alarm mode and a "mark this hotspot" feature. Calls to the response centre are recorded so threatening or abusive behaviour would be captured and could be used as evidence in court.

And this isn't just about runners. Anyone who goes off to do outdoor activities on their own could take advantage of it. We're thinking of people like horseback riders, hikers, mountain bikers, road cyclists and even people like hang gliders!
